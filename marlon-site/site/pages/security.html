Where your data lives

Marlon keeps a business’s details, products, customers and invoices on the phone, in the browser’s own storage. That is why it works with no internet. Nobody else can see what is stored on another business’s phone.

  • Solo businesses: invoices never leave the phone, unless you share them yourself (by WhatsApp, email, a QR code or a printed receipt) or send one for signature.
  • Teams: each invoice is saved on the rep’s phone first, then sent to your company’s boss dashboard when there’s internet. See Team invoices.
  • Backups: the owner’s dashboard can save a backup file of everything on the phone, to keep somewhere safe and restore later.

Locked phones

  • Every buyer, boss and rep gets a personal link. Opening Marlon without one is blocked.
  • A link works on only the number of phones paid for. For a rep, that is one phone: the first to open it. Passing the link on does nothing.
  • When online, each phone checks that it is still switched on. A phone that has been switched off is blocked at its next check and can’t send invoices.
  • The boss can switch off a rep straight from the boss dashboard. Their invoices stay under their name, and their place can be given to a new rep. The old phone can never come back on it.
  • On iPhone, Marlon asks to be added to the Home Screen first. Safari and a Home Screen app keep separate storage, so this stops one iPhone from using two places.

Passcodes and PINs

  • Boss dashboard: the boss sets their own passcode on the first visit. It works on up to two boss phones. After five wrong tries in 15 minutes, it waits.
  • Owner’s dashboard on the phone: locked with a PIN that is stored only as a salted PBKDF2-SHA-256 hash (210,000 rounds). After five wrong tries the phone waits 30 seconds, and the wait doubles after each further wrong PIN.
  • Payment terms stay private: cash, 7 days or 14 days are chosen on a page only the owner sees, and never appear on the customer’s copies or the signing screen.

Encryption

  • In transit: everything between the phone, the boss dashboard and our server goes over HTTPS.
  • Shelf orders (Pro): the orderer’s name and signature are encrypted on our server with AES-256-GCM before they’re stored.
  • Send for signature: the frozen invoice copy, the buyer’s name and their signature are encrypted on our server with AES-256-GCM. The secret link (256-bit random) is never stored, only a hash of it. Signing checks the copy’s SHA-256 fingerprint, so nobody can change a sent copy, and a signed copy can’t be sent again or cancelled. Unsigned links expire after 7 days.
  • Signing alerts: push alerts are encrypted end to end (RFC 8291, signed with VAPID), and the alert subscriptions are kept encrypted on our server.
  • Customer QR page: the whole invoice travels inside the link, after the “#”, a part of a web address that browsers never send to a server.

Team invoices

For a team, invoices and Marlon Pro credit notes are kept on our server so the boss dashboard can show them. They are reached only through your boss’s passcode-locked dashboard, and each rep’s phone only sends its own invoices. For invoices and credit notes, the store’s signature picture stays on the rep’s phone; only “signed: yes or no” is sent. Shelf orders are the exception: the orderer’s name and signature are sent so the boss and drivers can see who ordered, and they are stored encrypted.

  • The boss can’t write or edit invoices. The boss’s only changes are marking an invoice paid and managing reps.
  • A rep’s phone can never undo “paid”.
  • Team invoices are stored by our hosting provider, Cloudflare. They are not encrypted separately by Marlon. The encryption described above applies to send-for-signature copies, signatures and alert subscriptions.
  • We don’t look at, sell or share your invoices. The maker’s console we use to manage buyers doesn’t show invoice contents.

What reaches our server

  • Activation and checks: the personal link, a random id for the phone, the type of phone and browser, and the days it was seen.
  • Anonymous counts (you can turn these off in Settings): a random id plus “what happened, which day”, such as an invoice made or a receipt printed. Never names, customers, products, prices or amounts. Nothing is sent before setup is finished.
  • Team invoices and, with Pro, credit notes and shelf orders, as described above. A shelf order includes the name and signature of the person who placed it; both are stored encrypted (AES-256-GCM) and the boss sees the signature only on request.
  • Invoices sent for signature, encrypted.
  • Shelf photos with Marlon Pro, passed on to be read. See below.

Marlon Pro and AI

To read a shelf photo, Marlon sends the photo (shrunk on the phone to about 200 KB) and your company’s product names to Google’s Gemini service. Our server doesn’t keep the photo after it has been read. The phone keeps a small copy in its own history. Google handles the photo under its own Gemini API terms. Please don’t photograph people or private papers. Shelf photo is only available with Marlon Pro, and only when a rep chooses to take one.

Hosting

Marlon and this website run on Cloudflare Workers, with data stored in Cloudflare Durable Objects. Pages and the app are served over HTTPS only.

Limits we want you to know

  • Offline means offline. A switched-off phone that stays offline keeps working until its next check with internet.
  • Clearing the browser clears Marlon. Data is kept in the phone’s browser storage. If the browser’s data is cleared or the phone is lost, invoices not yet sent to the boss are lost unless you have a backup. Make backups from the owner’s dashboard.
  • A new phone is a new device. A new phone, or a browser whose data was cleared, takes a new place. Switch off the old one to free its place.
  • The lock is strong, not magic. Our server never activates more phones than a link allows, but someone technical who copies the app’s files could get around the lock on their own phone.
  • AI can be wrong. Shelf photo counts can be wrong. Check the shelf before relying on them.

Certifications

Marlon doesn’t hold security certifications such as SOC 2 or ISO 27001, and we won’t claim otherwise. Instead, we explain exactly how it works on this page. If your company has a security questionnaire, send it to us and we’ll answer it in writing.
