How it works

From your sales history to a 3-month buying plan

Draxly learns from what your customers actually bought, checks itself against your own recent months, and gives your buyers forecasts and reorder suggestions they can trust.

1. Bring in your sales

Connect your system once and Draxly pulls sales every night: Oracle NetSuite, SAP S/4HANA, Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central, QuickBooks Online, Shopify or Square. Any other system can send data through the API, drop a CSV over HTTPS, or publish a nightly file at a web address. You can also upload a CSV or Excel export at any time.

When you upload a spreadsheet, it's read in your browser. Only monthly totals are sent to Draxly.

2. Draxly tests itself on your numbers

Before you rely on anything, Draxly hides your last 3 months, predicts them from the months before, and compares its prediction with what really happened. You see an accuracy score for your total, for every product, and for every store, warehouse and region.

3. A forecast for every product and location

Draxly forecasts the next 3 months for every product at every store or warehouse. These add up by region and in total, so every view agrees with every other one.

4. Reorder suggestions and alerts

Add stock on hand, stock on order, lead times, case packs and minimum orders, from a connection, the API or a file. Draxly then works out days of cover, safety stock and a reorder point for each item, and suggests what to order and how much. Low-stock alerts go out by email.

5. Every night, and every week

With a connection, new sales arrive every night at about 2 a.m. in your time zone, and the accuracy test and forecasts refresh on their own. Team members can also choose to get a weekly forecast report by email.

The forecasting method

The best method for each product, chosen on your own data

Products don't all behave the same. A steady staple, a seasonal drink and a fast-growing new line need different treatment. Draxly tries several proven forecasting methods on each product and keeps the one that best predicted that product's own recent months:

  • Recent average, for steady products
  • Trend, for products that are growing or shrinking
  • Smoothing with a damped trend, so a recent jump isn't projected forever
  • Seasonal patterns, using last year's same months, once there are 2 or more years of history

Forecasts never go below zero, and the total is always the sum of its parts.
